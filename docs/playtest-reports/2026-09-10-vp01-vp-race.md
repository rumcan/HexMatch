# VP-01 playtest — can anyone actually reach 10★? (5 seeds, real 144×144 map)

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Ticket:** `docs/HexMatch-tickets.md` → *VP-01 — Victory Points from Paving, and a Better AI Opponent*
**Design note:** `docs/VP-01-victory-from-paving.md`

The ticket changes the win condition from "first to 12 connections" to "first to
10★, where a paved-from-dirt road tile is 0.25★ and a post-setup plant is 1★".
That is a much more expensive scoreboard — 40 paved tiles at 4 Ore each — so the
one question worth measuring was **whether a game can end at all**, and the second
was whether the rival is in the race or watching it.

## Method

Headless races, one per map seed, driving the **real modules** — no game code
mocked, and **both seats** played by the shipped AI (`src/iso/ai.ts`), so the
numbers describe the opponent the player meets, not a strawman:

- opening Factory: `chooseRivalFactorySpot`, per seat, on the real terrain;
- every build turn, in the game's order: plant → `aiBuildStep` (A* road planning
  against `BUILD_COSTS` + `priceDepot`) → `planUpgrades`/`executePaves` → banking;
- income: `playerResources` trickle with the PP-07 fractional carry. The match-3
  board is **not** simulated, so every figure here is a *lower bound* on scoring
  speed — the wired game pays the player far more cargo for the same roads (J1);
- trading: the real `bankTrade` 4:1 bank, two exchanges per turn, aimed at the
  smaller shortfall between the next Depot and the next four paves — the rule
  `rivalBankTowardPlan` in `src/iso/game.ts`;
- scoring: `rescore` after each turn that built something, then `hasWon`.

Runs live in `tests/unit/iso-vp-race.test.ts` and are re-runnable by hand:

```
VP_RACE_MINUTES=45 VP_RACE_SEEDS=1337,7,42,99,2024 npx vitest run tests/unit/iso-vp-race.test.ts
```

The default in `npm test` is one seed and a 24-minute window (≈50 s, and every
seed below had won by then); the loop stops the moment a seat wins, so a faster
rule finishes faster.

**Every seed produced a winner, in 12.4–20.1 minutes of a 45-minute window, and
one seat won by a single point or less twice.** The margin column matters more

pottering. Seed 42, minute by minute:

Reading:

- **Paving starts immediately and so does scoring**: the slowest first point
  across all ten seats was 0.9 minutes, and seven of ten scored in the first build
  turn (0.1 m). The rival's own first point landed at 0.3–0.9 m on every seed. A
  rule that only fires in the late game would have shown a flat opening.
- **Points arrive in batches, and the plateaus are the Ore, not the AI.** Seed 42
  sits on 4.5★ for six minutes and 9.5★ for seven. A four-tile pave costs 16 Ore,
  Ore comes out of exactly one industry, and a busy seat only trades on the turn
  it cannot build — so a seat waits for a full batch, then jumps 0.75–1.75★. This
  is the intended rhythm (a batch of paving is a decision, not a drip) but it is
  also the single most "could be smoothed" thing in the data.
- **The loser always had a real game**: the trailer finished between 5★ and 9.25★
  — never 0★, never under half the scoreboard.
- **Ore is the scoreboard**: winners burned 132–160 Ore on pavement (40 paves ×
  4, ±a plant-heavy seat that needed fewer tiles). Nothing else in the game asks
  for that much Ore, which is what makes the trade-off bite.
- **10★ is a bit high for a *pure* pave race, but not for a real game.** These
  numbers are an economy-only floor: no match-3 income, no town roads, no
  blockade income tax. A player who is matching gems on the board converts road
  tiles into Ore several times faster — and the same tables show how front-loaded
  the score already is: seed 1337's leader held **6.75★ at minute 8**, two thirds
  of the way to the win, on trickle income alone. Expect a human to finish near
  the low end of the range or under it.
- **The win lands on or just past 10★**: seed 2024 finished 10.25★ because a plant
  and a pave batch landed in the same turn and `hasWon` is checked once per turn.
  Deliberate — the banner prints the true total; what is forbidden is the check
  lagging (a seat sitting on 13★ because the gate is only read at the top of a
  turn).

## What this measurement changed in the code

Neither the pacing nor the numbers were the problem; the AI's *wallet* was.

1. **`rivalBankTowardPlan` now banks toward the scoreboard, not just the next
   Depot.** The first run, before this change, ended 40 minutes with the leader on
   4.25★ and one seat on **0★ for the entire game**: every affordable turn was
   spent on roads and depots, and the 4:1 bank only ever existed on idle turns
   aimed at `DEPOT_COST`. Ore is the only cargo that scores, it comes from one
   industry, and 160 of it is the bill — so a rival that never trades toward Ore
   never scores at all. It now compares the shortfall to its next Depot against
   the shortfall to four paves and aims at whichever is closer.
2. **A rival that is *busy* also banks toward paving** (`rivalBankTowardPave`).
   After fix 1, seed 2024's rival still finished on 6.5★ having laid 27 gravel
   tiles it never paved: banking only happened on turns where nothing was
   affordable, and a seat with spare Wood is never idle — it would rather pay for
   its fortieth dirt tile than buy the Ore that turns thirty of them into points.
   It now converts into Ore on any turn its pave plan was blocked by price, and
   never sells a cargo the Depot plan still needs, so it cannot starve the build
   step it competes with.
3. **The harness's own refund bug**, fixed in the same pass: a planned tile the
   other seat had already taken is skipped by `executePaves`, and the game refunds
   the difference. The sim was charging the plan and keeping the debt, which drifts
   every later affordability answer.

## Findings worth keeping (no code change)

- **Rough ground is a real scoring veto, and it can be territorial.** A paved Road
  cannot cross rock, so a network that lives entirely in hills next to its Ore
  Mines *cannot score at all*, no matter how rich its owner is. On seed 2024 that
  is exactly where the rival ended up: 6.5★, 27 gravel tiles, no legal pave among
  them. The AI does not yet prefer flat ground when it plans a Depot; the VP-per-Ore
  weighting biases it that way but cannot outvote income. Left as a documented
  consequence (a player in that spot should re-route, and the inspector says so)
  rather than patched by giving paving a rough-ground discount — follow-up below.
- **`paveCandidates` is the cheapest thing in the game, and the AI knows it.**
  One upgraded tile inside a live gravel component flips the whole depot line to
  ×1.6 throughput for 0.25★, because `buildAllComponents` marks a component paved
  if *any* of its tiles is. The rival exploits this (first point, first turn); the
  in-game help now tells the player, so the mirror does not look like a bug.
- **`VP_TARGET` and `VICTORY` live in `src/iso/config.ts`, not in `victory.ts`.**
  Both race failures this session were the harness importing `VP_TARGET` from the
  module that *uses* it — `undefined`, and vitest blaming the value: *"expected
  value must be number or bigint"*. `npm run typecheck` does not read
  `tests/unit`, so nothing catches it. Grep the `export` line before importing in
  a test.

## Follow-ups this playtest suggests

1. **Batch smoothing**: a seat is idle-looking for six minutes at a time. It is a
   banking-rate artefact, not a pricing one — `planUpgrades` already sizes a batch
   to the purse (`floor(spare Ore / 4)`), so a seat with 5 Ore does buy one tile.
   What it cannot do is convert faster than **two 4:1 trades per turn**, and only
   on the turns its pave plan is price-blocked. Raising that cap while a pave
   batch is the goal — or dropping `keepOre` when a rival owns more gravel than it
   can ever pave — would smooth the curve. Balance-affecting; needs another pass
   with the board wired in.
2. **Rough-ground awareness in Depot planning** (`aiBuildStep` industry choice), so
   the rival does not build networks it can never score with.
3. **Re-measure with the match-3 board** — the floor in this report is an economy
   floor; the shipped player is much richer than the seat in these tables, and the
   target should be re-checked against a human before it is called final.
