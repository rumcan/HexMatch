# AI-01 playtest — rival difficulty presets, calibrated on AI-vs-AI races

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Ticket:** user request — "Upgrade the AI rival": easy/hard difficulty, a rival
that uses the market every so often, and — the core complaint — a rival that is
no longer passive (does not use the main roads, does not expand).
**Report on the VP-race harness this builds on:**
`docs/playtest-reports/2026-09-10-vp01-vp-race.md`

The calibration mechanism the user asked for: **two AI seats play against each
other, and the speed at which they reach the win points (10★) is how hard the
rival is**. Both seats run the shipped policy — same turn order
(plant → depot(`aiBuildStep`) → pave(`planUpgrades`/`executePaves`) → bank →
market) and same costs — so no difficulty can "win by cheating"; a harder preset
only gets to act faster and more decisively.

The simulator is `tests/unit/helpers/race.ts` (shared with the VP-01 race
invariants), the assertions are `tests/unit/iso-skill-calibration.test.ts`, and
the whole thing is re-runnable by hand:

```
AI_RACE_SEEDS=1337,7 AI_RACE_MINUTES=30 npx vitest run tests/unit/iso-skill-calibration.test.ts
```

The map boots exactly as the live game boots it — towns' ring roads and the
inter-town public highways seeded first — milestones record every seat's time
to 1★/5★/10★, and the loop stops the moment a seat reaches 10★. All numbers
below are **lower bounds on a real game's speed**: no match-3 board income, no
Black Market tempo swings, no blockades.

## The presets (`src/iso/skill.ts`)

| lever | easy | normal | hard |
| ----- | ---: | -----: | ---: |
| buildMs (turn clock) | 14 000 | 9 000 | 5 500 |
| idleMs (retry after a no-op) | 5 000 | 2 500 | 1 500 |
| expandPerTurn (depot plans / turn) | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| paveTiles (max paves / pass) | 4 | 8 | 12 |
| bankBonus (extra 4:1 exchanges / decision) | −1 | 0 | +1 |
| offerEveryMs (market post cadence) | 90 000 | 60 000 | 40 000 |
| raidEveryMs (plant raid cadence) | 0 (never) | 120 000 | 90 000 |
| blockades | no | yes | yes |
| urgencyBias (ore-urgency multiplier) | 0.75 | 1 | 1.3 |

In the game the difficulty is a live switch: the top-bar selector
("🤖 Rival: Easy/Normal/Hard") re-reads the preset on the rival's next tick —
no restart — or pin a match from the URL with `?rival=easy|normal|hard`
(exactly like `?seed=`). The last choice persists in localStorage.

## Measured

All races are two AI seats on one map, factories placed by the engine's own
rule (seat 1 farthest-from-centre, seat 2 farthest-from-seat-1), no match-3
income. "you"/"ai" are the two chairs that rule produces; a race stops at the
first 10★, so a trailing total is a snapshot of a seat still in flight.

## Findings from running the races

1. **The chairs are not the same map.** The lane around a chair decides
   absolute pace far more than any preset knob: on 1337 chair 1 finishes at
   9.9-17.6m while chair 2 caps at ~5★; on 7 and 42 the fast chair is chair 2.
   A mirror's winner is whoever drew the fast chair — 1337 says "you" three
   times, 42 says "ai" three times. That is why the ladder is judged by
   **mirror finishing times** and why head-to-head pairings pool **both**
   orientations: "hard wins from every chair" is not a property the policy can
   own; "hard converts a lane better than easy" is. On the calibration seed
   that conversion gap is real: parked in the identical weak chair-2 lane, the
   hard seat out-scores the easy seat 8.75★ : 4.75★ while their chair-1 twins
   finish.

2. **The race stops when someone wins — read trailers proportionally.**
   42's hard mirror ends at 5.4m with the loser on 2★; that is not a stall, it
   is minute 5.4 of a working seat. The assertions floor the trailer at "still
   on pace for 5★ in 20 minutes" (`5 × winnerTime / 20m`), not at a flat 5★.

3. **Map density decides whether difficulty is legible.** On 99 every lane is
   short enough that all three presets finish inside 5.4-6.0m: the differences
   fall below the two chairs' lane noise (~±30 s), the mirror order inverts,
   and pooled head-to-head even tips to easy (21 : 17.25). On the sparse maps
   the same knobs separate cleanly. Difficulty is a claim about how a preset
   converts a lane; a lane with nothing to convert has no difficulty to
   measure. 99 stays in the table precisely so the edge is documented instead
   of cherry-picked away.

4. **Mid-flight snapshots can lie about slow lanes — measure them to their
   end.** 42's chair 1 first looked like the one lane where easy's patience
   converts *better* than hard's aggression (5.25★ vs 2★ when the fast chair
   ended the race at ~5.4m). Run to a full window, hard reaches 10★ at 14.1m
   in that chair — two minutes ahead of easy's identical chair (16.1m). The
   hard seat's 2★ was not a stall: it was mid-plan, twenty-one unpaid gravel
   tiles deep into legs whose Ore income lands later; when the network comes
   online it paves out in one continuous sweep. The danger signal to keep
   watching is a seat that gains nothing *over minutes* (Finding 2's
   proportional floor assertion), not one that is temporarily behind at a wall
   the fast chair built.

5. **The market works as a metronome, not as charity.** Offers post on the
   preset's clock (hard 40 s, easy 90 s) only when surplus exceeds plan-need
   plus the bank's 4-unit lot; take rates between two AIs on these sparse maps
   are low (0-3/game) because an AI taker refuses a deal that starves its own
   plan. Against a *human* taker the same offers are simply good rates (4:2
   beats the bank's 4:1) — which is the gameplay the ticket asked for: the
   rival visibly tries to trade, every so often.

## The two economy failures the harness caught

The race harness paid for itself before a single difficulty number was tuned:
watching seats stall in mirror matches exposed two faults in the shipped
rival's own turn, both reachable in the live game, both fixed in this ticket
(in `game.ts` and mirrored in the sim — the `AI-01:` comments mark the seams).

### F1 — two banks trading against each other (the 4:1 churn)

Each half of the turn had its own 4:1 bank pass: one sold spare cargo into Ore
when a pave milestone was close, one sold spare cargo into whatever the next
Depot plan lacked. With a pave milestone live, pass A bought Ore while pass B
counted Ore as "spare" and sold it back — a slow 4:1 tax on the seat's own
income, and plan starvation beside it. The harness showed it as seats that
paved 3-8 tiles, then idled the rest of the game on positive income. Fix,
shipped in both the live turn and the sim: while any pave goal exists, Ore
flows **in** only (`paveGoal`/`paveMilestone` guards the sell lists); the pave
bank never sells cargo the full depot plan still needs; no bank trades below
the plan's demand plus the bank lot.

### F2 — the plant-rush stall (the "passive rival" the user reported)

Step 1 of the rival's turn bought a Processing Plant the moment
`canAffordPlant` was true. A plant is 1★; it also costs a Depot's worth of
Wood+Stone and earns nothing itself. Measured pre-gate on seed 7, chair 1: the
hard seat planted **twice in its first minute** (16 Wood + 16 Stone), never
rebuilt the cleared purse into expansion income, and ended 30 minutes with
3 depots, 12 paves and 26-31 Gold it has no way to spend — 5★. Same lane, same
policy, slower clocks (easy): 3 depots inside minute one, 16 depots by
minute 13, 10.5★. The complaint that opened this ticket ("the AI is very
passive and doesn't seem to use the main roads and expand") was this in a live
game: the rival spends its expansion budget on trophy points.

Fix, in both the live turn and the sim: a plant buy must leave the plan the
seat is actually working toward funded — pacing toward a Depot, the full plan
target plus the plant price; paving, Ore for the ready paves (up to a plant's
worth). Not a knob: all presets agree on it, so the ladder stays clean.
Measured on the same trap lane afterwards: the hard seat escapes to 10★ @
15.5m when the race gives it the time (7, hard-you vs easy-ai).
